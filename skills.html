Python

My journey with Python began in 2020, and it has remained my absolute go-to programming language ever since. What started as learning basic scripts as a hobby turned into using it during my Computer Science GCSE and a passion for building software that solves tangible problems. Over the years, I have lost count of the number of applications, tools, and experiments I have brought to life using its versatile ecosystem. From password crackers to art projects, Python has allowed me to turn creative logic into functional reality. This continuous, hands-on practice has truly shaped how I approach software development, making me a more efficent developer.

CAD

My passion for 3D design started when I was young with TinkerCAD, which was a playground for learning the very basics of spatial geometry and assembling objects. As my creative ambitions grew, I moved to using Blender, exploring the world of meshes, modeling and rendering. Today, Fusion 360 is my primary software which helps bridge the gap between imagination and virtual objects. I use this advanced CAD software to design intricate parts, detailed prototypes and making the groundwork for my future projects.

3D Printing

Receiving a Bambu Lab A1 Mini 3D printer for my 16th birthday completely changed my hobby, finally allowing me to bridge the gap between virtual objects and physical reality. Up until I got a 3D printer my projects rarely lived outside the screen and if they did, they were made with cardboard, but having a high-precision machine on my desk instantly turned my CAD designs into touchable objects. This powerful tool has pushed me to focus on design quality, forcing me to consider variables like tolerances, structural integrity, and print orientation. Iterating through physical prototypes has massively improved my engineering skills, transforming the way I model parts and ensuring that every final print is both visually appealing and functional.

Robotics

The best outcome happens when I merge my coding knowledge with physical engineering, combining Raspberry Pis and Python with custom CAD-designed frames. This unique intersection of hardware and software allows me to build complete, functional robotic projects from scratch. There is nothing more rewarding than designing a chassis in Fusion 360, 3D printing it, and then writing the exact Python scripts needed to make it come alive. Watching a concept evolve from a line of code and a virtual blueprint into a physical machine acting by my instructions in the real world is incredibly fulfilling, and it drives me to keep creating new projects.
